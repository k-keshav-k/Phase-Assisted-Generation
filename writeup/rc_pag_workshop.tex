\begin{abstract}
Diffusion language models can reduce neural function evaluations (NFEs) by ending block
refinement early, but repeated locally plausible stops can accumulate into a large accuracy
loss. We introduce Risk-Calibrated Phase-Adaptive Generation (\method), a native-loop
controller that combines (i) a prompt-disjoint isotonic calibration of local trajectory-
disagreement risk, (ii) a persistent prompt-level risk ledger and stop cap, (iii) a learned
remaining-NFE gate, and (iv) exact next-step proposal verification before any forced commit.
Only three ledger policies are tuned. One policy per model is frozen before an untouched
500-prompt calibration set certifies the end-to-end harmful-regression probability---AdaBlock
is correct but \method is wrong---at $\alpha=0.02$, with Bonferroni familywise control across
LLaDA and Dream. Compute is deliberately not forced into a bounded-loss certificate: paired
NFE changes may be negative, so confirmation instead requires a preregistered paired-bootstrap
NFE-reduction lower bound above 5\% for each model. The hook is embedded in the exact native
AdaBlock loops and must reproduce AdaBlock bit-for-bit when it never stops. This draft inserts
numbers only from complete non-mock artifacts that pass the frozen coverage and claim gates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Masked diffusion language models (dLMs) generate many tokens in parallel by repeatedly
denoising a partially masked sequence \citep{nie2025llada,ye2025dream}. Their wall-clock
advantage depends on when tokens or blocks are committed. Fixed schedules spend refinement
steps uniformly; recent adaptive methods vary the block length, token transfer, or stopping
time using confidence and stability signals
\citep{lu2026adablock,wu2025fastdllm,israel2025apd,mohamed2025sched}.

The central difficulty is that confidence is not itself a safety guarantee. A scheduler
chosen because it looks efficient on a tuning set can silently over-commit on new prompts,
and searching many thresholds amplifies this selection bias. We therefore ask:
\emph{Can an adaptive dLM decoder minimize refinement compute while controlling the
probability of changing a full-refinement block?}

Our answer, \method, separates local prediction, prompt-level resource allocation, verification,
and end-to-end certification. A compact calibrated estimator predicts whether the current
proposal disagrees with the block's native full-trajectory endpoint. Rather than applying one
threshold independently at every block, the decoder spends predicted risk from a persistent
prompt ledger, limits the number of forced commits, and acts only when a remaining-NFE regressor
predicts worthwhile compute. A proposed stop remains revocable for one step and commits only if
every still-masked token keeps the same top-one proposal. Three predeclared ledger policies form
the entire family. Tuning freezes one per model before paired end-to-end calibration with AdaBlock.

Our contributions are:
(i) an exact AdaBlock-compatible stopping hook with a mandatory no-stop parity gate;
(ii) prompt-disjoint calibration of a high-signal local trajectory-disagreement model;
(iii) a persistent risk ledger, stop cap, benefit gate, and revocable exact-agreement commit;
(iv) finite-sample familywise control of end-to-end harmful-regression risk, with honest raw
paired-bootstrap compute evidence that permits negative prompt-level savings; and
(v) an auditable evaluation over LLaDA and Dream with paired accuracy, NFE, runtime,
regression risk, out-of-domain transfer, and explicit positive-claim gates.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Efficient dLM decoding.}
LLaDA and Dream establish competitive autoregressive-style reasoning with masked diffusion
objectives \citep{nie2025llada,ye2025dream}. Fast-dLLM uses confidence-aware parallel decoding
and cache reuse \citep{wu2025fastdllm}; AdaBlock adapts block size online
\citep{lu2026adablock}; APD learns token-wise denoising paths \citep{israel2025apd}; and SchED
uses similarity-based early stopping \citep{mohamed2025sched}. FreeDave, OSDT, and WINO
further explore lossless verification, one-shot thresholding, and revocable inference
optimization \citep{liu2026freedave,zhou2026osdt,wang2026wino}. DiCo and SOAR study
distillation and schedule optimization, respectively \citep{luo2026dico,cao2026soar}.
Very recent work predicts semantic block boundaries (SemBlock), combines confidence with
token and distribution persistence (LESS), or coordinates simultaneous commits through a
mean-field objective \citep{song2026semblock,mohamed2026less,zoabi2026meanfield}.
Stability-Weighted Decoding gives a temporal-distribution view of unsafe commits, STDec combines
spatial and temporal consistency, and Just-on-Time studies independent token convergence
\citep{wu2026swd,chen2026stdec,kohut2026jit}. Prophet commits early from answer-region confidence,
while the concurrent LATCH framework verifies sustained stability over a parsed candidate span
\citep{li2025prophet,lee2026latch}. We therefore use temporal JS only as an input feature and do
not claim stability itself as our contribution. These approaches motivate transparent controls,
but their main contribution is the efficiency rule rather than a joint accuracy--compute
certificate. Concurrent theory analyzes efficiency
conditions for confidence decoding \citep{cai2026confidence}; our guarantee instead treats
the decoder as a black box and controls an observed operational loss. A contemporaneous
re-evaluation also finds that confidence-based remasking gains can depend strongly on the
decoding setting \citep{frkovic2026reevaluating}, reinforcing the need for paired,
protocol-specific evidence.

\paragraph{Statistical risk control.}
Learn-then-Test (LTT) converts a fixed collection of predictors into risk-controlling choices
using valid hypothesis tests \citep{angelopoulos2021ltt}; conformal risk control extends this
perspective to general bounded losses \citep{angelopoulos2022crc}. We apply the LTT principle
to adaptive inference. Unlike ordinary post-hoc confidence intervals, simultaneous testing
remains valid after selecting the lowest-compute certified policy. RC-Jot concurrently applies
conformal risk control to dLM early exit in an automated technical report
\citep{fars2026rcjot}. In contrast, our calibration unit is a frozen native-AdaBlock policy and
our certificate controls the exact end-to-end harmful-regression event for a policy selected
before calibration; compute remains a separate preregistered paired estimand because realized
NFE changes are not bounded to $[0,1]$ prompt-wise.

\section{Risk-calibrated adaptive stopping}

\subsection{Problem and end-to-end harm loss}

Let $X\sim\data$ be a prompt. A block diffusion decoder creates blocks $b=1,\ldots,B(X)$.
Within block $b$, $S_{b,t}$ is the complete decoder state after refinement step $t$, and a
policy $\pi_\lambda$ chooses a stopping time
$\tau_b^\lambda\in\{1,\ldots,T_b\}$. Its compute is
\begin{equation}
 C_\lambda(X)=\sum_{b=1}^{B(X)}\tau_b^\lambda,
 \qquad \nfe(\pi_\lambda)=\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\data}[C_\lambda(X)].
 \label{eq:compute}
\end{equation}
Let $G_0(X)$ denote the complete AdaBlock generation and $G_\lambda(X)$ the generation under
the hooked stopping policy. If $A(Y,X)\in\{0,1\}$ is the task grader, define
\begin{equation}
 L_\lambda(X)=\indicator\!\left\{A(G_0(X),X)=1,\ A(G_\lambda(X),X)=0\right\},\qquad
 \risk(\lambda)=\Pr_{X\sim\data}[L_\lambda(X)=1],
 \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
This asymmetric loss counts only regressions on prompts that AdaBlock solves. It directly
matches the operational failure the acceleration must avoid while allowing benign sequence
changes and recoveries; we report both separately. Define the paired NFE reduction
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_\lambda(X)=C_\lambda(X)-C_0(X),\qquad
 \rho_\lambda=-\frac{\mathbb E[\Delta_\lambda(X)]}{\mathbb E[C_0(X)]}.
 \label{eq:saving}
\end{equation}
Unlike a clipped ``advantage'' label, $\Delta_\lambda$ preserves prompts on which instrumentation
or a changed trajectory uses more NFEs. Our feasibility objective is
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\lambda\in\family}\ \nfe(\pi_\lambda)
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad \risk(\lambda)\le\alpha.
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
The positive empirical headline additionally requires a lower confidence bound
$\rho_\lambda^{\rm L}>0.05$ for each model.

\subsection{Calibrated local risk and a prompt ledger}

The decoder compresses each logits tensor on device and transfers only scalar summaries:
masked fraction; block step and size; entropy, top-one probability, and top-one/top-two
margin quantiles; token churn; changes in these quantities; delimiter and special-token
rates; and temporal-instability summaries. No logits or hidden states are retained on CPU.
For masked position $j$, let $p_{t,j}=\mathrm{softmax}(\ell_{t,j})$. We measure normalized
temporal instability between consecutive native AdaBlock steps as
\begin{equation}
 d_{t,j}=\frac{\operatorname{JS}(p_{t,j}\,\|\,p_{t-1,j})}{\log 2}\in[0,1],\qquad
 \bar D_t=\frac{\sum_{j:\,m_{t,j}=1}d_{t,j}}{\lVert m_t\rVert_1},\quad
 D_t=\max_{j:\,m_{t,j}=1}d_{t,j}.
 \label{eq:temporal-js}
\end{equation}
For a native full-budget trajectory, let $z_{b,t}$ be the proposal at state $S_{b,t}$ and
$z_b^\star$ the block tokens after native refinement finishes. We label every observed state by
\begin{equation}
 Y_{b,t}=\indicator\{z_{b,t}\ne z_b^\star\},\qquad
 R_{b,t}=\max\{0,T_b-t\}.
 \label{eq:local-labels}
\end{equation}
Prompt groups, not states, are partitioned: 480 prompts fit a histogram-gradient-boosting
classifier and 120 held-out prompts fit an isotonic map. This yields calibrated local score
$p_{b,t}=\widehat q(S_{b,t})\in[0,1]$. A separate regressor fit on all 600 prompts predicts
$\widehat R(S_{b,t})$, the remaining native block NFEs. The label is local on purpose: earlier
experiments found it highly predictable on both models, whereas direct prompt-harm heads suffered
severe on-policy label shift. End-to-end safety is established later on untouched prompts.

For policy $\lambda=(B,K,g)$, let $Q$ be risk already spent on verified commits in the current
prompt and $k$ their count. A state is eligible when
\begin{equation}
 t\ge2,\qquad Q+p_{b,t}\le B,\qquad k<K,\qquad \widehat R(S_{b,t})\ge g.
 \label{eq:stopping-rule}
\end{equation}
The first eligible state stores its proposed top-one tokens but does not commit. The next
eligible state commits only if every position that remains masked has the same proposal;
otherwise the pending proposal is replaced and verification restarts. Pending state is reset
at each block, while $(Q,k)$ persists across blocks and resets only between prompts. On a verified
commit, and only then, $Q\leftarrow Q+p_{b,t}$ and $k\leftarrow k+1$. We tune only
\begin{equation}
 (B,K,g)\in\{(0.02,1,4),(0.05,2,3),(0.10,3,2)\}.
 \label{eq:ledger-family}
\end{equation}
For each model, the accuracy-eligible candidate
with the lowest tuning NFE is frozen. Calibration therefore evaluates two already-selected
model--policy pairs and performs no threshold search.

\subsection{Finite-sample calibration}

Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be an untouched calibration sample and
$K_\lambda=\sum_i L_\lambda(X_i)$. There is one frozen policy for each model, hence $J=2$
simultaneous harm hypotheses. The null $H_\lambda:\risk(\lambda)\ge\alpha$ uses
\begin{equation}
 p_\lambda=\Pr_{Z\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(n,\alpha)}[Z\le K_\lambda].
 \label{eq:harm-pvalue}
\end{equation}
Candidate $\lambda$ is certified iff $p_\lambda\le\delta/J$. Both model-specific policies must
certify before confirmation begins. We report simultaneous Clopper--Pearson harm upper bounds.
Calibration also reports raw paired $\Delta_\lambda$, including negative savings, but does not
mislabel a bootstrap interval as a finite-sample risk certificate.

\begin{proposition}[Selection-safe end-to-end harm control]
Assume the calibration prompts are exchangeable draws from the deployment distribution,
the model and decoding protocol are fixed, and $\family$ is independent of calibration
outcomes. With probability at least $1-\delta$ over calibration, every policy certified by
Equation~\ref{eq:harm-pvalue} satisfies $\risk(\lambda)\le\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\textit{Proof sketch.}
For any unsafe candidate with $\risk(\lambda)>\alpha$, the lower tail of its binomial count is
stochastically no larger than the boundary distribution, so its test rejects with probability
at most $\delta/J$. A union bound over the two frozen model policies completes the claim.
Appendix~\ref{app:proof} gives details.

\paragraph{Calibration cost and deployment cost.}
Local surrogate labels are amortized from one full trajectory per training prompt.
Calibration executes paired AdaBlock and candidate generations because the certified loss
requires complete graded answers. At deployment, feature extraction is
$O(V)$ for the already-materialized vocabulary logits and $O(d)$ retained scalars, while the
CPU estimator cost is negligible relative to a model forward pass.

\section{Experimental protocol}

\paragraph{Models, data, and splits.}
We pin LLaDA-8B-Instruct and Dream-v0-Instruct-7B revisions and use deterministic decoding
with generation length 256, at most 64 refinement steps, and block length at most 64.
Estimator training, tuning, and calibration use disjoint deterministic subsets of GSM8K,
MATH, and MBPP training data. The 500-prompt calibration mixture per model is never used for
fitting or threshold search. The completed v1 evaluation is treated only as development
evidence. Workshop confirmation draws its deterministic complement: 500 GSM8K and 200 MATH-500,
100 sanitized MBPP, and all 64 remaining HumanEval problems; HumanEval is declared
out-of-domain. Proportional strata spread each sample across the unused benchmark rows. The
frozen YAML, dataset revisions, seeds, prior-sample exclusions, and protocol hash accompany
the code.

\paragraph{Comparators and ablations.}
The confirmatory comparison includes AdaBlock, the per-model best accuracy-eligible
nonlearned rule chosen on tuning data, and the frozen certified \method policy. The two
development controls are clearly labeled style reproductions of distribution-stability and
token-convergence stopping rules; they are not claimed as official implementations of prior
methods. Every control uses the same AdaBlock hook. The calibrated local-risk model is evaluated
with Brier score/AUROC on its prompt-grouped calibration fold; the remaining-NFE regressor is
evaluated with mean absolute and root-mean-square error on the same held-out prompt groups.

\paragraph{Metrics and inference.}
Primary metrics are mean NFE, exact task accuracy, elapsed time, harmful regressions, and
sequence disagreements.
Every method receives the same prompt order and decode settings. We report paired bootstrap
95\% intervals for NFE and accuracy differences (10,000 resamples), normalized cross-model
NFE, simultaneous calibration bounds, and a failure taxonomy. The positive headline is
allowed only if: (1) both non-mock model policies certify harm; (2) \method lowers NFE
versus AdaBlock on both models and versus the best nonlearned rule overall; (3) each
in-domain accuracy-difference lower bound versus AdaBlock is at least $-0.02$; and (4) the
pooled in-domain paired-bootstrap NFE-reduction lower bound exceeds 5\% for each model.

\paragraph{Compute-aware funnel.}
We first run 32 prompts/model and require exact generated-token, NFE-history, and block-history
parity between native AdaBlock and a never-stop instrumented hook. Only if this gate passes do
we collect 600 training traces/model, fit the prompt-disjoint calibrated-risk model and
remaining-NFE regressor, and tune on a fresh 150 prompts/model. Calibration on 500
prompts/model is launched only when an accuracy-eligible policy saves at least 8\% NFE and
beats the best eligible nonlearned control on \emph{each} model. Native-loop v4/v5 full-budget
traces with complete temporal evidence may be reused, but all old heads, rollout labels, policy
selections, certificates, and confirmation results are discarded. This matters because development
diagnostics exposed two distinct failures: v4 accumulated safe-looking local stops on Dream, while
v5's direct prompt-harm head had held-out AUROC 0.456 on Dream and 0.372 on LLaDA. Confirmation
cannot start without an explicit flag and both harm certificates. All records are atomic and
resumable; models are loaded
one at a time on one A100.

\section{Limitations and broader impact}

The certificate is distribution- and protocol-specific. Dataset shift, a different model
revision, stochastic temperature, altered block size, quantization, or a modified prompt
template requires new calibration. The asymmetric harm loss protects only answers AdaBlock
gets right; it does not guarantee correctness, factuality, fairness, harmlessness, or output
identity. The calibration distribution is a held-out training-task mixture, so the finite-
sample certificate applies to that mixture; benchmark-wise confirmation remains empirical.
Paired calibration increases experimental compute. Bonferroni correction is
simple and robust but conservative, especially for correlated policies. The evaluation uses
two open dLM families and reasoning/code tasks; it should not be generalized to all models,
languages, or domains. Faster generation can lower energy per response but may also increase
aggregate use. We release exact compute and failure records to make both effects measurable.

\section{Conclusion}

\method reframes adaptive diffusion decoding as risk-constrained prompt-level resource allocation.
It combines a calibrated high-signal local model, a persistent risk ledger, a learned benefit
gate, revocable exact-agreement verification, and a selection-safe finite-sample test for
end-to-end harm. Compute improvement is evaluated separately with paired raw NFEs and a frozen
bootstrap gate. The result is a decoder that either deploys a pre-frozen harm-certified policy
or falls back transparently. The frozen
evaluation is designed so the strength of the paper follows the evidence rather than the
other way around.

\section{Proof details}
\label{app:proof}

Fix an unsafe candidate $\lambda$ with $r=\risk(\lambda)>\alpha$. Under the assumptions,
$K_\lambda\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(n,r)$. The binomial lower tail is nonincreasing in $r$, so
the rejection region defined by $p_\lambda^H\le\delta/J$ has probability at most $\delta/J$.
A union bound over the two frozen model policies yields familywise error at most $\delta$.
If either test fails, confirmation is not started and no harm-control assertion is made.

\section{Online algorithm}

At each prompt, \method initializes $(Q,k)=(0,0)$. For each AdaBlock block it repeats:
(1) execute the unchanged native denoising step; (2) summarize logits, distribution change,
and token change on device; (3) obtain calibrated risk $p_{b,t}$ and predicted remaining NFE;
(4) check Equation~\ref{eq:stopping-rule}; (5) store the first eligible proposal without
committing; and (6) force-commit only when the next eligible state agrees at every still-masked
position, then charge $p_{b,t}$ to $Q$ and increment $k$. With a never-stop hook,
the original token transfers, cache updates, block boundaries, and model calls are unchanged.
Calibration runs this complete candidate trajectory and a paired AdaBlock trajectory, then
records Equation~\ref{eq:loss} from their task grades.

\section{Reproducibility checklist}

The artifact records repository and dataset revisions, the config and protocol hashes,
prompt IDs, raw outputs, correctness, NFE, elapsed time, per-block compact traces, selected
policy, parity and readiness audits, harmful regressions, raw paired NFE changes, harm calibration
$p$-values and simultaneous bounds, paired confirmation intervals, and every failed
claim gate. Code execution grading uses a separate subprocess with a timeout. Secrets are
read from the environment and never written to the command line or artifacts. Exact local
and Slurm commands are provided in \texttt{docs/rc\_pag\_runbook.md}.
